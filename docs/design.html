Experimental design

This page follows the second paper of the project. We want to reconstruct a function across its domain from few measurements. Our method addresses two separate limitations: a geometric preference for the boundaries, and sampling decisions that ignore the measured values. The example below separates their effects.

1. Different functions, unchanged sampling

Three rules measure the same function from the same 10 starting points (a Sobol sequence). Variance measures where the model is most uncertain. Equalizer multiplies the variance by a precomputed weight that downweights the preference for the edges. Equalizer and warp also computes the variance in coordinates that are stretched where the measurements so far suggest that the function changes quickly.

Switch functions at the same number of measurements. Variance and the equalizer keep their locations; the warp recomputes its sequence from the observations.

Correcting the boundary bias changes the geometric preference, but the sequence still ignores what was measured. The warp lets the measurements change where we measure next. It only sees the measurements collected so far, not the true function.

The reconstruction fills in every location with the value of its nearest measurement, and the error compares it with the true function. This is one run with noisy measurements, so the order of the curves can change between functions and starting points. The paper reports averages over many runs and 22 benchmarks.

2. What the warp changes

We construct the warp from gradients of the model's prediction. Regions assigned a higher density occupy more space in the transformed domain, which changes the correlations used to choose the next measurement.

The warp changes the geometry the acquisition rule works in. It does not simply give steep regions a higher score.

The views follow the run above. The density never drops below a uniform level, which limits how strongly the points can concentrate. The points do not follow the density exactly.

The score with equalizer and warp

a(x) = gN(x) · σ²T(x)

The equalizer gN(x) = min(1, vN(centre) / vN(x)), where vN is the posterior variance averaged over reference designs of N points. It is precomputed for the required design sizes and does not depend on the measurements.

ρ(x) = 1 + ‖∇μ(x)‖ / c   (in two dimensions)

The density ρ uses the gradient of the posterior mean μ, and c is the root-mean-square gradient magnitude over the domain. The map T transports the normalized density to the uniform density, and σ²T is the posterior variance of a Gaussian process on the warped inputs T(x).

3. Why the acquisition rules favour different locations

An acquisition function gives every location a score, and the next measurement goes where the score is highest (the ring). With fixed model settings, the score of these three depends on where the earlier points are, but not on the values measured there. So we can ask where each of them wants to measure without any measured values.

One set of points shows one choice. Repeating it over many random sets shows the preference of each acquisition function. The maps below show how often the next measurement lands at each location.

never often "At the edge" means closer than 0.05 to it. This band occupies 19 % of the domain's area.

Variance maximization sends the next measurement to the corners and edges. The two integrated criteria avoid the edge itself and prefer a band inside the domain. No measured values are used, so this preference comes from the model and the shape of the domain alone.

The three scores

The model has posterior variance σ²(x) and posterior covariance c(x, x′) after the measurements so far, and the noise variance is τ².

Variance: a(x) = σ²(x)

NIPV: a(x) = average over x′ of c(x, x′)² / (σ²(x) + τ²)

EPIG: a(x) = average over x′ of −½ log(1 − ρ(x, x′)²)

Here ρ(x, x′) is the correlation between a noisy measurement at x and one at x′, and x′ runs over the whole domain. None of the three contains a measured value. The paper has the derivations and the same maps for other kernels and dimensions.
